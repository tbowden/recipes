
Vanilla, Baking, and Icing Reference Guide


🍦 Vanilla Extract Strategy

• Pantry Storage Only: Never refrigerate pure vanilla extract. Cold temperatures and condensation cause alcohol evaporation and dull the aromatics.
• The "Raw" Bitterness Illusion: Pure vanilla contains 35%+ alcohol and concentrated plant compounds. Tasting it straight from the bottle will taste sharp, medicinal, and bitter. The sweet flavor only unlocks when diluted with sugar, fat, or dairy.
• The Dual-Bottle Strategy:
	• Imitation Vanilla Flavouring (Vanillin): Use for baked cakes, cookies, and muffins. The vanillin molecule resists oven heat beautifully, whereas pure vanilla evaporates in the oven.
	• Pure Vanilla Extract: Reserve strictly for unheated elements like royal icing, buttercream, whipped cream, puddings, and glazes.

🎨 Food Colouring & Icing Guide


Supermarket Liquids vs. Gels

• Liquid Food Colours: Best reserved for pastel shades only. Because they are water-based, adding enough liquid to create deep colors will ruin royal icing consistency and cause buttercream to split/curdle.
• Gel Food Colours: Essential for deep, vibrant colors (like Christmas Red and Green). Brands like Queen Food Colour Gel or Over The Top Gel Paste are widely available in supermarket baking aisles.
• Crucial Rule: Avoid oil-based colorings (e.g., Colour Mill) in royal icing, as the fat will break down the egg white structure. Oil-based gels are excellent for buttercream, however.

Professional Hacks for Deep Christmas Red & Green

1. The Time Trick (Most Important): Red and green pigments take hours to fully oxidize. Mix the icing until it reaches a dark pink or grassy green, cover tightly, and let it sit on the counter for 2–4 hours. It will naturally mature into a deep crimson or forest green.
2. Color Neutralization:
	• For True Red: Buttercream has a natural yellow tint that turns red into coral/orange. Add a microscopic dot of blue or purple gel to cool down the yellow and force a deep, rich berry-red.
	• For Pine Green: Add a tiny drop of black, dark brown, or blue gel to your green icing to knock back neon brightness.
3. The Microwave Melting Trick (Buttercream Only): If gel colors look dull in buttercream, scoop out 1 cup of the frosting and microwave for 4–5 seconds until slightly melted and glossy. Stir it back into the main batch; the gentle heat instantly dissolves and activates the gel crystals, darkening the whole batch.

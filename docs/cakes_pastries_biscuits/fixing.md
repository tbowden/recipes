# Moist Chocolate Cake: Fixing Uneven Baking

## Problem
Large-volume batter in a large light aluminium round tin (wedding-cake base-layer size). At 180°C conventional the edges cook well before the middle. A smaller round tin cracked badly; the larger tin reduced cracking but caused the edge/centre gap.

**Last result:** ~4cm deep at edges, ~6cm in the centre (centre lagging, then rising late).

## Cause
Oven too hot for the batter volume. The edges set and dry out while the centre is still liquid. The centre then either domes and cracks or lags behind.

## Plan: next trial
- **Temperature:** 160°C conventional, middle shelf (a 20°C drop from 180°C)
- **Time:** expect ~1.5-2 hrs; start testing at ~75-80 min
- **Damp towel strips** pinned around the outside of the tin to slow the edges
- **Flower nail or metal skewer** standing upright in the centre of the batter to carry heat to the middle
- **Double-line the sides** with baking paper, extending above the rim
- **Don't open the door** for the first 45-50 min (the centre can sink)
- **Doneness:** skewer in the centre, aiming for a few moist crumbs
- **Rest** in the tin 15-20 min before turning out

## How to adjust next time
| Observation | Change |
|---|---|
| Edges still firmer/drier than the middle | Drop another 10-20°C |
| Centre still much higher than the edges (dome) | Go cooler |
| Even cake but slightly dry edges | Towel strips or flower nail rather than temperature |
| Edges still racing ahead at 160°C | Try 140°C |

## Temperature limits
- **Practical floor: ~130°C conventional.** Below that, the crumb can turn dense, gummy or sunken, and bakes run 3+ hrs.
- If using fan, drop a further 15-20°C.
- Change one thing at a time in 10°C steps.

## Other options
- Split the batter into two thinner layers, or a tin plus a few cupcakes.
- Use a ring/tube tin, which heats the middle structurally.
- Check the oven with a thermometer, since many home ovens are 10-20°C off.

## Notes
- Mum's version was a deeper cake in a large square tin and worked perfectly. Likely differences: her oven/temperature and thicker tin sides shielding the edges.
- Tin: light-coloured, heavy-gauge aluminium is the most forgiving.
